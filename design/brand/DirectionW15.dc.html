Why
One typeface, one colour, one size, two weights. No border, no symbol, no accent, no second file — the whole mark is a font-weight change at the hyphen, which means it can be set in an email signature, a contract header or a Word document and still be correct. It is the cheapest possible identity to own and the hardest to get wrong, and the weight change puts the stress on “Frames”, which is the half of the name people remember.

Tradeoff
It is barely a logo. This is a styling convention rather than a mark, and it will not be recognised without the name being read — there is nothing to spot at a distance, nothing to put on a case badge, and no square version. It is also the most common wordmark solution in existence, so it is distinctive in the way a good business card is distinctive: not at all, just correct.
